How each setup is defined

Reversal

Based on MCD (+349%) and DAL (+253%). In the last 7 days RSI hit 33 or lower and the close sat 5% or more below the 50-day SMA. Triggered: MACD crossed above its signal in the last 2 days. Turned: crossed 3 to 7 days ago, histogram still positive, RSI under 60 and close still at or below the 50-day. Watch: histogram below zero but rising. Early: oversold and histogram still falling.

Momentum

Based on AAPL (+116%). Pullback in an uptrend: RSI 45 to 60, MACD above zero and above its signal, close 0 to 3% above the 50-day SMA.

Squeeze

Bollinger bandwidth in the tightest 20% of the last 6 months, close within 3% of the 20-day high and above the 50-day SMA.

Avoid

Based on UBER (−26%). RSI 38 to 46, MACD histogram flat near zero, close 4% or more below the 50-day SMA. A slow drift where time decay eats calls.

Only stocks above $10 with at least $100M average daily dollar volume are scanned. A stock that fits more than one setup shows only the first, in the order above. These are rule matches, not predictions or advice.

How the estimate works

Uses 2026 federal brackets, standard deductions and capital gains thresholds from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32. Options held one year or less are short-term and taxed like wages; held longer, they get long-term rates (0%, 15% or 20%). Short- and long-term results are netted the way Schedule D does it. A net loss offsets up to $3,000 of other income ($1,500 if married filing separately), and the rest carries over to 2027.

The 3.8% net investment income tax applies to gains above $200,000 of income ($250,000 married filing jointly, $125,000 separately). State tax is a flat approximation at the rate you enter. The estimate doesn't include AMT, credits, phase-outs, or Section 1256 contracts such as SPX index options, which get 60/40 treatment.

Wash sales: if you close at a loss and buy the same or a substantially identical position within 30 days before or after, the loss is deferred. Trades that look like that are flagged; enter the disallowed amount from your 1099-B to apply it.

This is an estimate to help you plan and set money aside, not tax advice. Your broker's 1099-B is the official record. Check with a tax professional before you file.
